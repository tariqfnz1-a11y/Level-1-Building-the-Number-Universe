3. Fractions and decimals: sharing a pizza

The denominator says how many equal pieces. The numerator says how many pieces we take.

4. Rational numbers: anything you can write as p/q

A number is rational if it can be written as p/q with p and q integers and q ≠ 0. So 5 = 5/1, -3 = -3/1, 0.5 = 1/2, 0.75 = 3/4.

Decimals come in two kinds: terminating (0.5 stops, like a runner who says "I'm finished") and recurring (0.333... keeps going round the track). Both are rational. Try different pieces in the pizza above: a denominator like 3, 6, 7 or 9 gives a recurring decimal.

Remember: every integer is rational, but not every rational number is an integer. Every car is a vehicle, but not every vehicle is a car.

5. Irrational numbers: the song that never repeats

√2 = 1.41421356237... goes on forever and never settles into a repeating block. So do √3, √5, √7 and π.

Golden rule: the square root of a perfect square is rational (√4 = 2, √9 = 3, √25 = 5). The square root of a non-perfect square is irrational.

6. Recurring decimal lab: your first algebra trick

Enter the repeating digits (1 to 3 digits). Example: 3 for 0.333..., or 45 for 0.4545...

Rule: 1 repeating digit, multiply by 10. 2 digits, by 100. 3 digits, by 1000. The repeating "wagons" cancel when you subtract.
